============================================================
1. PURPOSE AND SCOPE
============================================================

This runbook defines how NexaCore identifies, contains, investigates, communicates and closes information-security incidents. It applies to workforce identities, endpoints, cloud infrastructure, applications, customer-facing services, data stores and approved third-party systems.

A security event becomes a security incident when available evidence indicates an actual or reasonably suspected compromise, policy breach, unauthorized access, malicious activity, data exposure or material security control failure.

============================================================
2. SEVERITY MODEL
============================================================

SEV-1 CRITICAL
Examples:
- Confirmed widespread compromise of production infrastructure.
- Confirmed exposure of Restricted customer data at material scale.
- Active credential theft affecting privileged identities.
Target acknowledgement: 5 minutes.
Target incident commander assignment: 10 minutes.
Executive notification target: 30 minutes.

SEV-2 HIGH
Examples:
- Confirmed unauthorized access to a sensitive production system.
- Malware affecting multiple managed endpoints.
- Significant security-control bypass with credible exploitation.
Target acknowledgement: 15 minutes.
Incident lead assignment: 30 minutes.

SEV-3 MEDIUM
Examples:
- Confirmed compromise of a single non-critical endpoint.
- Suspicious activity with limited confirmed impact.
- Policy violation with potential security consequences.
Target acknowledgement: 1 hour.

SEV-4 LOW
Examples:
- Low-risk policy deviation.
- Benign alert requiring investigation.
Target acknowledgement: 1 business day.

Severity may be raised as evidence develops.

============================================================
3. FIRST 15 MINUTES
============================================================

The first responder should:

1. Record the alert/event time.
2. Create or update the security incident ticket.
3. Preserve available logs and evidence.
4. Avoid unnecessary changes to the suspected system.
5. Determine whether credentials should be revoked.
6. Notify the Security Operations lead for SEV-1/SEV-2.
7. Identify affected users, systems, accounts and data.
8. Record actions and timestamps.

Do not delete suspicious files or logs merely because they appear malicious. Preserve evidence according to the investigation procedure.

============================================================
4. TRIAGE QUESTIONS
============================================================

The incident lead should establish:

- What happened?
- When did it start?
- What evidence confirms the event?
- Which identities are involved?
- Which systems are affected?
- What information could have been accessed?
- Is the activity still occurring?
- Is there evidence of lateral movement?
- Is a third party involved?
- What controls can safely reduce exposure?

Initial impact categories:

A. Identity compromise
B. Endpoint compromise
C. Application compromise
D. Cloud/infrastructure compromise
E. Data exposure
F. Third-party security incident
G. Policy/control violation

============================================================
5. CONTAINMENT
============================================================

Containment actions may include:

- Revoke or suspend compromised credentials.
- Disable suspicious sessions.
- Isolate affected endpoint(s).
- Restrict network paths.
- Block malicious indicators.
- Pause affected integrations.
- Increase logging for impacted systems.
- Restrict administrative access.

Containment should minimize evidence destruction and avoid unnecessary customer impact.

For privileged credential compromise, Security Operations should prioritize token/session revocation and credential rotation.

============================================================
6. INVESTIGATION AND EVIDENCE
============================================================

Potential evidence includes:

- Identity-provider authentication events
- Endpoint detection telemetry
- Application access logs
- Cloud audit logs
- Network telemetry
- Database audit events
- Email security records
- Security-tool alerts
- Relevant configuration history

Evidence should have a recorded source, collection timestamp and incident reference.

Investigators should distinguish:
1. Confirmed facts
2. Strong indicators
3. Working hypotheses
4. Unknowns requiring further investigation

Avoid presenting assumptions as confirmed findings.

============================================================
7. DATA EXPOSURE ASSESSMENT
============================================================

When data exposure is suspected, determine:

- Data classification.
- Data owner.
- Approximate number of records.
- Time window.
- Whether data was accessed, copied, modified or merely exposed.
- Whether encryption or other controls reduced impact.
- Customer/contractual obligations.
- Legal or regulatory notification requirements.

The Security lead coordinates with Legal, Privacy and the relevant business owner where required.

============================================================
8. COMMUNICATION
============================================================

Internal incident communications should include:

- Incident severity
- Current impact
- Confirmed facts
- Actions completed
- Actions in progress
- Customer/business impact
- Next decision point
- Named incident owner

Do not speculate about attribution or disclose unverified technical details.

External communications must be approved through the designated Legal/Communications process. Engineers and individual responders must not independently notify customers, regulators or media.

============================================================
9. INCIDENT COMMAND
============================================================

For SEV-1 incidents, the standard command structure is:

Incident Commander
  |
  +-- Security Lead
  +-- Technical Lead
  +-- Communications Lead
  +-- Business/Customer Lead
  +-- Scribe

The Incident Commander owns coordination and decision flow. The Technical Lead owns technical containment/recovery decisions. The Scribe maintains the timeline.

============================================================
10. RECOVERY
============================================================

Recovery should include:

1. Confirm containment.
2. Remove persistence or malicious access.
3. Rotate affected credentials.
4. Restore systems from trusted state where required.
5. Validate security controls.
6. Monitor for recurrence.
7. Return services gradually when practical.

Production recovery should follow approved deployment and change procedures.

============================================================
11. POST-INCIDENT REVIEW
============================================================

A formal review is required for SEV-1 and SEV-2 incidents and for any incident specifically requested by Security leadership.

Review target: within 3 business days of stabilization.

The review must include:

- Executive summary
- Timeline
- Customer/business impact
- Root and contributing factors
- Detection performance
- Containment actions
- Recovery actions
- Control gaps
- Corrective actions
- Owners
- Target dates

Blame-oriented language should be avoided. The objective is to improve systems and controls.
