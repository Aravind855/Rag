### **1.2 Core employment principles**

- Employment decisions must use documented role criteria, performance evidence and approved compensation bands.

- Managers may not create local rules that contradict a company policy without written People Operations approval.

- Employee records are maintained in the HRIS; spreadsheets or email copies are not authoritative once the HRIS record is updated.

- Business-critical exceptions must have an owner, expiry date and written rationale.

- The employee handbook is a baseline; team-specific operating procedures may add controls but may not remove mandatory requirements.

### **1.3 Internal terminology**

**Core Hours:** 10:00–16:00 IST. **Workday:** 8 productive hours excluding meal breaks. **Business Day:** Monday–Friday excluding company holidays. **People Manager:** employee with formally assigned direct reports. **Critical Role:** role designated by the function head because absence can materially affect customer, security or revenue commitments.

### **2.2 Attendance controls**

• Employees must record attendance through the approved HRIS attendance event or approved mobile check-in where assigned.

- A missed punch must be corrected within 2 business days. Corrections older than 7 days require manager plus People Operations approval.

- Three uncorrected attendance exceptions in a rolling 30-day period trigger a manager review.

- Employees scheduled for customer or production support must remain reachable throughout the assigned roster.

- Working outside the assigned location for more than 5 consecutive business days requires manager approval and may require tax/payroll review.

### **2.3 Lateness and availability**

A delay of more than 30 minutes during a scheduled workday should be communicated before the start time where reasonably possible. Repeated lateness is evaluated using the attendance record rather than isolated events. For remote employees, availability is measured by agreed working hours, meeting attendance, response expectations and completion of assigned work; presence indicators alone are not treated as proof of productivity.

### **2.4 Time-off handover**

For planned absence of 2 or more business days, the employee should publish a handover containing active work items, deadlines, escalation contacts, customer commitments and access dependencies. Critical-role employees must also identify a named backup.

## **3. Performance, Probation and Internal Mobility**

### **3.1 Performance cycle**

|**Cycle**|**Timing**|**Required output**|**Owner**|
|---|---|---|---|
|Goal setting|First 15 days of cycle|3–6 measurable goals|Employee + manager|
|Monthly 1:1|Monthly|Progress, blockers, feedback|Manager|
|Mid-cycle review|Month 6|Written review + rating direction|Manager|
|Year-end review|Month 12|Final rating + development plan|Manager + calibration|
|Promotion review|Quarterly window|Evidence against level rubric|Manager + panel|


### **3.2 Probation**

- Standard probation is 6 months from joining date.

- At approximately day 75, the manager documents an initial progress check covering delivery, collaboration and role readiness.

- The formal month-3 review identifies whether expectations are on track, at risk or materially below role requirements.

- The month-6 decision is recorded as confirmed, extended or not confirmed, subject to applicable employment terms.

- Probation extensions require People Operations review and a written development plan with a maximum initial extension of 3 months.

### **3.3 Promotion evidence**

Promotion is not based solely on tenure. The evidence pack must show sustained performance at the next-level scope, business impact, decision quality, role complexity and demonstrated behaviors. A manager should normally provide at least two completed examples from the preceding six months for L2–L4 progression and three examples for L5+ progression.

### **3.4 Internal transfer**

Employees with at least 9 months in their current role may normally apply for an internal opening. Exceptions are allowed for organizational restructuring, critical skill shortages or documented career-development plans. A transfer date should allow at least 10 business days for operational handover unless the two managers agree otherwise.

## **4. Conduct, Communication and Workplace Standards**

### **4.1 Communication standards**

- Email and chat should identify the required action, owner and due date when a task is being assigned.

- Urgent production or security matters must use the designated escalation channel rather than relying on email alone.

- Meeting invitations should include an agenda for meetings longer than 30 minutes.

- Decisions affecting more than one team should be recorded in the approved project or knowledge system within 1 business day.

- Customer or partner commitments must not be communicated as confirmed unless the responsible function has accepted the commitment.

### **4.2 Workplace conduct**

NexaCore expects professional conduct in office, remote and customer environments. Harassment, intimidation, retaliation, deliberate misrepresentation of work records, unauthorized disclosure of confidential information, falsification of expense or attendance records, and misuse of company systems may lead to disciplinary action.

### **4.3 Conflicts of interest**

Employees must disclose actual or potential conflicts involving vendors, customers, competitors, hiring decisions or financial interests. A disclosure does not automatically mean misconduct; the objective is to establish a documented mitigation plan. People Operations maintains the disclosure register and assigns a review owner.

## **5. Leave, Travel and Employee Records**

### **5.1 Leave administration**

- Planned leave should be requested through the HRIS before the start date wherever practicable.

- Managers are expected to respond to leave requests within 2 business days.

- Unplanned absence must be communicated to the manager or designated team channel as soon as reasonably possible.

- Leave balances are calculated from the HRIS ledger; local spreadsheets are reference-only.

- Employees changing teams retain their accrued statutory/company leave unless a policy specifically provides otherwise.

### **5.2 Business travel**

|**Travel item**|**Standard limit**|**Exception owner**|
|---|---|---|
|Domestic hotel|INR 6,500/night|Function head|
|Domestic meals|INR 1,200/day|Manager|
|Local transport|Actuals with receipt|Manager|
|Air travel|Economy for flights < 4 hrs|Function head for exception|
|International hotel|City-band rate card|Finance|


### **5.3 Employee record changes**

Changes to legal name, bank details, emergency contact, tax declarations or identity documentation must be made through the HRIS workflow. Payroll-impacting changes received after the monthly cutoff may be applied in the following payroll cycle. Bank-account changes are subject to verification and are never accepted solely through an email request.

### **5.4 Exit administration**

Exit processing includes manager handover, asset return, access revocation, expense settlement, benefits closure and final payroll review. For involuntary exits or security-sensitive departures, access revocation may be coordinated with the employee's last working minute. People Operations maintains the separation checklist and completion evidence.

### **6.2 Mandatory employee actions**

- Complete annual information-security and code-of-conduct training by the assigned deadline.

- Keep emergency-contact and bank information current in the HRIS.

- Report suspected policy violations through the approved reporting channel.

- Protect company and customer information regardless of work location.

- Use only approved systems for storing restricted employee, customer and financial data.
