# **NexaCore Sales Playbook**

### **Version 4.1 | Enterprise Sales Execution**

**Owner:** Revenue Operations **Effective:** 01 Aug 2026 **Review:** 31 Jul 2027

## **1. Sales Operating Model**

This playbook defines NexaCore's synthetic enterprise sales process, qualification standards, forecasting practices and account-management controls. It is designed as private company data for RAG evaluation and is not sourced from a real company.

### **1.1 FY26 commercial snapshot**

|**Metric**|**FY26 value**|**Definition**|
|---|---|---|
|Qualified pipeline|INR 184.6 crore|Open opportunities at qualified stages|
|Closed-won bookings|INR 71.8 crore|Signed bookings recognized by Sales Ops|
|Win rate|31.4%|Won opportunities / closed opportunities|
|Average enterprise deal|INR 42.6 lakh|Median segment varies by region|
|Sales cycle|74 days|Qualified opportunity to close|
|Renewal rate|88.2%|Renewed eligible contracts|
|Net revenue retention|113%|Existing customer cohort|


### **1.2 Sales stages**

- Prospect: account identified and basic fit established.

- Discovery: business problem, stakeholders, budget context and timeline documented.

- Qualified: MEDDICC-style qualification meets minimum evidence.

- Proposal: commercial proposal issued with approved pricing.

- Negotiation: commercial/legal terms actively negotiated.

- Closed Won/Lost: contract outcome recorded with reason code.

## **2. Qualification and Discovery**

### **2.1 Qualification minimums**

|**Field**|**Required evidence**|
|---|---|
|Business problem|Documented problem and business impact|
|Economic buyer|Named or explicitly unknown with plan to identify|
|Decision process|Known or active discovery|
|Budget|Budget range, funding path or commercial qualification|
|Timeline|Target decision/implementation date|
|Competition|Known competitor or status quo|
|Technical fit|Architecture/security requirements identified|


### **2.2 Discovery discipline**

- Discovery calls over 45 minutes should have an agenda and named attendees.

- Customer requirements must be recorded in CRM within 1 business day.

- A verbal statement such as 'we love the product' is not treated as qualification evidence.

- Security, procurement and legal requirements should be identified before final proposal where applicable.

- Opportunity stage changes require evidence, not optimism.

### **2.3 Disqualification**

Opportunities should be removed or recycled when there is no identified problem, no plausible decision path, a timeline beyond the planning horizon without a committed next step, or a solution mismatch.

## **3. Pipeline Management and Forecasting**

### **3.1 Forecast categories**

|**Category**|**Probability guide**|**Required evidence**|
|---|---|---|
|Pipeline|<20%|Early discovery / limited evidence|
|Best Case|20–49%|Qualified need + active next step|
|Commit|50–79%|Economic buyer + commercial path + dated next step|
|Closed|100%|Contract/signature or approved booking evidence|


### **3.2 Forecast rules**

- Commit deals must have a dated customer next step inside the current forecast period.

- Sales managers review pipeline coverage weekly.

- A forecast change above INR 25 lakh should have a documented reason.

- Opportunities without activity for 21 days are flagged as stale.

- Quarter-end deals require explicit legal/procurement status before entering Commit.

### **3.3 Pipeline coverage**

Reference coverage target is 3.0× quarterly quota for enterprise teams and 2.5× for mature account books. Coverage is evaluated against qualified pipeline rather than unqualified lead volume.

## **4. Enterprise Sales Motion**

### **4.1 Standard sequence**

- Account research and stakeholder mapping.

- Discovery and qualification.

- Solution workshop / technical validation.

- Security and architecture review where applicable.

- Business case and commercial proposal.

- Procurement and legal negotiation.

- Executive alignment.

- Signature, handoff and implementation kickoff.

### **4.2 Proof of value**

Proof-of-value engagements should define success criteria before technical work starts. The standard duration is 14–30 calendar days. Extensions beyond 30 days require Sales Engineering and the account executive to document why the extension is commercially justified.

### **4.3 Handoff to Customer Success**

|**Handoff item**|**Required timing**|
|---|---|
|Signed contract + commercial terms|Within 1 business day|
|Implementation scope|Within 3 business days|
|Success metrics|Before kickoff|
|Known risks / commitments|Before kickoff|
|Customer stakeholders|Before kickoff|

## **5. Account Management and Commercial Hygiene**

### **5.1 Account review cadence**

- Tier-1 accounts: monthly account review.

- Tier-2 accounts: quarterly business review.

- Expansion opportunities must have a separate opportunity record rather than being hidden inside renewal notes.

- Customer escalations affecting renewal risk must be linked to the account record.

- Discounts must follow the separate Discount Policy.

### **5.2 Renewal risk signals**

|**Signal**|**Risk flag**|
|---|---|
|Usage decline >20% over 30 days|High|
|Executive sponsor lost|High|
|Renewal date <90 days with no plan|High|
|Open critical support issue|High|
|Contract value dispute|Medium|
|No active user engagement|Medium|


### **5.3 Commercial hygiene**

Every open opportunity must have owner, stage, amount, close date, next step, customer, product scope and forecast category. CRM is the system of record; personal spreadsheets do not override CRM.

### **6.2 Governance**

- Weekly forecast call: deal movement, risk and next steps.

- Monthly pipeline inspection: stage aging, coverage and conversion.

- Quarterly territory review: capacity, whitespace and account concentration.

- Revenue Operations audits CRM completeness monthly.

- Commission eligibility uses approved booking and payout records, not informal sales spreadsheets.
